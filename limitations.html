Limitations and implications

What these results can and cannot support

Each limitation below follows from the design or from a check that is still outstanding. None is hypothetical; the numbers are the study's own.

Sample size

31 case firms rather than the 40 the design targeted: no further qualifying cases exist in the enforcement records searched. The layer tests use 16–25 informative matched sets, so non-significant results are weak evidence of no difference; the simulated power at an odds ratio of 1.5 per standard deviation is about 70 % with 21 sets.

Observational design

Every test is an association within matched sets. Nothing here shows that related-party transactions cause fraud, or that a preventive approach beats a reactive one.

Matched-sample assumptions

Matched firms are firms with no SEC accounting enforcement, not firms known to be clean; a matched firm with a high score may be a false alarm or an undetected case. Three sets are poorly size-matched because their industries contain few firms of the case's size; results are reported with and without them.

Measurement quality

The eight characteristics were coded by rule with the evidence sentence kept, not by a language model reading the filings, and the reliability check against human coding (M2) has not been done. Amounts are stated in about a fifth of firm-years and about three times as often by case firms, so the financial layer is tested on a subset selected on the outcome. Texts that state "no transactions" score as incomplete, which explains part of the H2 reversal.

Multiple testing

H1–H3 are Holm-adjusted; H4 runs fifteen year-by-layer tests with 7–25 sets each and is read as limited evidence; the firm-clustered standard errors the design asks for are about twice the model-based ones.

Retrospective association, not prospective prediction

The five-year window usually contains the years of the misconduct. The study therefore observes firms while the problem is under way and before the public knew, not before the misconduct began. "Earliest separation" is a year inside the window, never advance warning, and the 5 % false-alarm threshold is fixed by construction, not an accuracy result.

The exploratory model

The fitted 36-input model contains the audit and internal-control signals, so it cannot test whether the RPT layers add to them; its out-of-fold discrimination is reported for information. It was fitted before the 31st case was added.

Implications

For theory, the monitoring idea of agency theory finds some support in the governance layer, the conflict-of-interest idea only where amounts are disclosed, and the information-asymmetry idea none in the disclosure measures as designed; the competing efficient-transaction prediction is the reading for the disclosure layer. For practice, disclosed related-party characteristics as measured here do not add to the signals auditors already produce. For method, every value remains traceable to a filing sentence, which is what makes the human check (M2) and any re-coding straightforward.
