Date: 8 October 2026. Inputs: SEC-EDGAR-Downloader/scripts/phase_a_screen_v2.csv (51 screened candidates), scripts/candidates_v02.csv (58 raw candidates), cases_core.csv (final sample), the peer-matching files scripts/peers_matched_*.csv. Rules: decisions D1–D4 and D10 of docs/PROJECT_CLOSURE_PLAN.md, which implement the client's research design of 6 October 2026.

1. What the screen had to decide

The research design needs case firms whose SEC enforcement action involved related-party transactions (RPTs), observed for the five fiscal years before the misconduct became public, each matched to industry-and-size peers that were never subject to an enforcement action. Four rules were applied to every candidate, in this order:

  1. D1 inclusion. The SEC order or complaint must describe an accounting misstatement or a misappropriation in which an RPT is the mechanism (class misstatement, misappropriation, or a combination). Cases where the only charge is a missing or incomplete RPT disclosure are a separate stratum (disclosure_only). Cases with no RPT element or with an internal-controls-only order are excluded.
  2. D2 revelation date. The first public event that revealed the problem: an 8-K Item 4.02 non-reliance notice, an auditor resignation, an audit-committee investigation, a short-seller report, a restatement, or, when nothing earlier exists, the SEC action itself. Every date carries a confidence level (high = dated SEC filing or order; medium = documented public event still to be linked to a source document; low = SEC action date used as a placeholder).
  3. D3 window and four-report rule. The assessment window is the five fiscal years before the revelation year. A case must have at least four annual reports (10-K, 10-K405, 10-KSB, 10-KT) in that window, and the window must start in 2005 or later so that Item 9A (SOX 404) exists and Layer 2 is observable.
  4. D4 peer matching. Five peers per case from the EDGAR universe of the case's industry (same 4-digit SIC, falling back to 3- and 2-digit), nearest in log total assets in the fiscal year before revelation, with a 10-K in every window year and no AAER match.

Size match: median absolute gap in log total assets 0.40 (a factor of 1.5), interquartile range 0.07–0.91. Three clusters are poorly size-matched and must be flagged in the thesis: Ocwen (mean gap 5.4 log points — the mortgage-servicing SIC has almost no registrants of Ocwen's size), Energy XXI (3.8) and Singularity (2.8). A robustness run without these three clusters is pre-specified in the closure plan.

Three cases have only three peers (Ocwen, MusclePharm, Puda Coal) because the industry pool with full 10-K coverage in the window is thin. Puda Coal's size anchor is hand-collected from its FY2010 10-K (XBRL did not cover it); American Equity's peers are anchored on 2011 assets because the XBRL frames begin in 2009 and its window is 2005–2009.

6. Implications for the thesis and the paper

  • Sample size. The design asked for 40 core cases; 21 exist under its own rules after an exhaustive search of every AAER (2005–2024) and litigation release (2010–2025). Section 12 of the design anticipated the shortfall. The thesis should restate power for 21 cases × 5 peers (about 105 case firm-years against 525 peer firm-years) and treat the 17-case sensitivity set (three-report rule) and the 13-case extended tier (pre-2005 windows; financial and text layers only) as robustness analyses, not as part of the confirmatory test.
  • Generalisation ("many more companies"). The way to test the model on many companies is not to add them as labelled cases — there are no more verified RPT cases — but to score the labelled-free EDGAR universe of the 33 industries (12,615 registrants; 4,611 with XBRL assets) and report the external alert rate at the 5 % clean-firm false-positive threshold (decision D10). That is the thesis's generalisation evidence and it is already supported by the universe files built in Phase A.
  • Heterogeneity. With 21 cases across 18 industries, the within-cluster peer comparison is what makes the test meaningful; pooled cross-industry comparisons should not be the headline.
  • Disclosure-only stratum. Report separately; do not merge into the core count.

7. Readiness verdict

The candidate screen is complete and reproducible (scripts/phase_a2_revelation_dates.py regenerates the screen; scripts/build_cases_core.py and scripts/finalize_phase_a.py regenerate the sample). The sample is fit to build the dataset and to run the confirmatory model. It is not yet fit to publish: the 6 low- and 9 medium-confidence revelation dates and the two open D1 confirmations must be closed, and the three poorly size-matched clusters must be carried as a documented limitation with a robustness run.
