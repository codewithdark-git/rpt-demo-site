Results

The score overlaps; governance leans the predicted way; disclosure leans the other

50 case firms, 237 matched firms, 1411 firm-years. Each finding states the question, the answer, the chart, how to read it and the numbers behind it.

The two risk-score distributions overlap substantially

Question. Does the rank-averaged risk score separate case firm-years from matched firm-years?

Finding. No. The case median is a little higher, but the histograms sit on top of each other and only 10 of 50 cases ever cross the matched firms' 95th percentile.

How to read. Density per group (left axis), box plots with median and interquartile range, and every firm-year as a dot. The dashed line is the threshold fixed at the 5 % matched-firm rate. Use the controls to look at one event year or one layer.

Firm-years without any observed characteristic have no score and are not drawn (1411 analysed, 1329 scored).

Five hypotheses, one reading each

Status uses the pre-registered rule: predicted direction and Holm-adjusted p < 0.05 with firm-clustered standard errors. "Not supported" means the data did not show the predicted difference at that standard; it does not show the opposite unless the direction is marked as such. Odds ratios are per one standard deviation of a 0–1 rank score; statistical significance is not practical importance.

Within matched sets, only governance points the predicted way and clears the model-based test

Question. Is each layer higher in case firm-years than in the same set's matched firm-years?

Finding. Governance: odds ratio above one with both error types, significant only with model-based errors. Financial: above one on the disclosing subset, not significant with firm-clustered errors. Disclosure: below one, the opposite of the prediction.

How to read. Points are odds ratios per standard deviation on a log axis; the line at 1 means no difference. Circles use model-based errors, squares the firm-clustered errors the design asks for; intervals that cross 1 are not significant. A firm's five window years are not independent, which is why the clustered intervals are about twice as wide.

The governance gap is present in every window year and does not grow towards disclosure

Question. Are the differences present in the earlier years, not only the final year (H4)?

Finding. With the conditional logit as the primary test, no year before the final year is significant for any layer; governance comes closest at t−3. A stable gap between firms fits the picture as well as fraud emerging.

How to read. Left: mean layer score of case firms (solid) and matched firms (dashed) by event year. Right: the per-year odds ratio with its 95 % interval. Years are fiscal years counted back from the revelation year, not calendar years.

The combined score does not add to the audit and internal-control signals

Question. Does the risk score add to the traditional index (material weakness, audit opinion, auditor change, restatement), in strength or in timing (H5)?

Finding. Model B beats Model A out of set, but both are below the traditional index used alone; the score's coefficient is not significant with firm-clustered errors; at an equal false-alarm rate the score flags no case the traditional rule does not.

How to read. Left: out-of-set AUC (0.5 = chance). Right: cases flagged by each rule at the same false-alarm rate among matched firm-years.

The sample moves precision, not direction

What was varied. The 50-case main sample with the version-3 coder against the 31-case sample of 10 October 2026 with the version-2 coder.

Finding. No layer changes sign between the two samples; the larger sample narrows the intervals.

What each case's score is made of

Question. Which of the eight measured characteristics rank high for which case firm?

Finding. Amounts are rarely stated, so most scores rest on the two disclosure items and CEO duality.

How to read. Each cell is the case firm's mean 0–1 rank over its window years (darker = higher); empty cells are characteristics never observed for that firm. The last row is the mean over all matched firm-years. Ranks are measured filing characteristics; the final column is the derived score.
