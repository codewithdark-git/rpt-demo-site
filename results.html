Results and hypotheses

The score overlaps; governance leans the predicted way; disclosure leans the other

31 case firms, 145 matched firms, 862 firm-years. Each finding below states the question, the answer, the chart, how to read it, the numbers and the caveat. All numbers are in the Excel workbook; the full chapter has every table.

The two risk-score distributions overlap substantially

Question. Does the rank-averaged AI risk score separate case firm-years from matched firm-years?

Finding. No. The case median is a little higher, but the histograms sit on top of each other and only two of 31 cases ever cross the matched firms' 95th percentile.

How to read. Density per group (left axis), box plots with median and interquartile range, and every firm-year as a dot. The dashed line is the threshold fixed by construction at the 5 % matched-firm rate. Use the controls to look at one event year or one layer.

Caveat. Firm-years without any observed characteristic have no score and are not drawn (862 analysed, 813 scored).

Status uses the design's rule: predicted direction and Holm-adjusted p < 0.05 with firm-clustered standard errors. Statistical significance is not practical importance; odds ratios are per one standard deviation of a 0–1 rank score.

Within matched sets, only governance points the predicted way and clears the model-based test

Question. Is each layer higher in case firm-years than in the same set's matched firm-years?

Finding. Governance: odds ratio above one with both error types but significant only with model-based errors. Financial: above one on the disclosing subset, not significant with firm-clustered errors. Disclosure: below one, the opposite of the prediction.

How to read. Points are odds ratios per standard deviation on a log axis; the line at 1 means no difference. Circles use model-based errors, squares the firm-clustered errors the design asks for; intervals that cross 1 are not significant.

Caveat. The financial layer is observed in a subset selected on the outcome (amounts are stated about three times as often in case firm-years).

The governance gap is present in every window year and does not grow towards disclosure

Question. Are the differences present in the earlier years, not only the final year (H4)?

Finding. With the conditional logit as the primary test, no year before the final year is significant for any layer; governance comes closest at t−3. A stable gap between firms fits the picture as well as fraud emerging.

How to read. Left: mean layer score of case firms (solid) and matched firms (dashed) by event year. Right: the per-year odds ratio with its 95 % interval; years are fiscal years counted back from the revelation year, not calendar years.

Caveat. Fifteen tests with 7–25 informative sets each; the observations precede public disclosure, not necessarily the misconduct.

The combined score does not add to the audit and internal-control signals

Question. Does the AI risk score add to the traditional index (material weakness, audit opinion, auditor change, restatement), in strength or in timing (H5)?

Finding. Model B beats Model A out of set, but both are below the traditional index used alone; the score's coefficient is not significant with firm-clustered errors; at an equal false-alarm rate the AI rule flags no case the traditional rule does not.

How to read. Left: out-of-set AUC (0.5 = chance). Right: cases flagged by each rule at the same false-alarm rate among matched firm-years.
