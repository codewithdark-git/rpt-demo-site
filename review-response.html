Response to the researcher's review of 9 October 2026

RPT fraud-risk study — design tests re-run exactly as the design and the review specify · 10 October 2026 · research assistant

This document answers every red note and every numbered instruction in RPT Research Design – 06.10.26 – REVIEWED against RA report 09.10.26. Nothing in the agreed design was changed: the research question, objectives, hypotheses, the eight characteristics, the scoring steps and the AI risk score are as written on 6 October. The tests were re-run on all qualifying RPT-fraud cases with the design's own rank-averaged AI risk score. Everything built on the fitted 36-input score is now in a separate, labelled exploratory section (§6). The researcher approved Gulfport Energy as the 31st case on 10 October; the main sample is therefore 31 cases. One thing still needs the researcher's and supervisors' decision before any result is final: the M2 threshold and human coding (§5).

1. Summary of the honest results (31 cases, design rules applied)

  • Sample. 31 RPT-fraud cases (21 misstatement/misappropriation + 10 disclosure-only including Gulfport Energy, all FRAUD = 1) with 145 matched firms in 31 sets; 517 firm-years in the largest layer test, 862 window firm-years in all (152 fraud firm-years).
  • H1 (financial exposure): not supported by the design rule with model-based errors; not supported with the firm-clustered errors the design asks for — OR 1.61 per SD [1.04, 2.49], as predicted; p = 0.031, Holm p = 0.062; firm-clustered p = 0.206 (Holm = 0.459); 187 firm-years, 63 firms, 17 sets.
  • H2 (disclosure less complete and vaguer): not supported by the design rule with model-based errors; not supported with the firm-clustered errors the design asks for — OR 0.80 per SD [0.63, 1.02], in the OPPOSITE direction; p = 0.075, Holm p = 0.075; firm-clustered p = 0.374 (Holm = 0.459); 517 firm-years, 117 firms, 25 sets. The design's second run without the disclosure-only cases gives OR 0.72 (p = 0.042). H2 is reported as not supported; no re-wording.
  • H3 (weaker governance): supported by the design rule with model-based errors; not supported with the firm-clustered errors the design asks for — OR 1.70 per SD [1.22, 2.37], as predicted; p = 0.002, Holm p = 0.006; firm-clustered p = 0.153 (Holm = 0.459); 284 firm-years, 71 firms, 18 sets. The layer is carried by CEO duality (board share observed in 15% of firm-years).
  • H4 (differences in earlier years): with the conditional logit as the primary test, the design rule (a significant year before the final year) is met for: no layer. The closest is governance in year −3 (p = 0.088). Fifteen tests with 7–21 informative sets each: limited evidence either way.
  • H5 (layers add to audit and internal-control signals): not supported for the design's AI risk score. AI_SCORE is not significant in Model B with firm-clustered errors (p = 0.296); Model B's out-of-set AUC (0.52) is above Model A (0.45) but below the traditional index alone (0.60); at an equal false-alarm rate the AI rule flags 0 cases and is earlier than the traditional rule in 0, later in 3; 28 of 31 cases are flagged by neither rule.
  • P1: reported as a pattern only (§4.7). M1 coverage is now reported by group: a related-party amount is stated in about three times as many fraud firm-years as matched firm-years, so the H1 subset is selected on the outcome (§4.1). M3 met. M2 not done (workbook delivered, §5).
  • A1–A3 re-done on the design score: every assessment is named evidence (A1); re-ranking without each set in turn changes the largest layer in 1.0% of firm-years (A2); in the fraud firm-years the AI rule flags, the largest layer is L2 — and the disclosure layer runs against its prediction (A3, §4.8).
  • Reading. On the design as written and on the data as coded, the governance layer separates fraud firms from their matched firms, the financial layer does so only on the small disclosing subset, and the disclosure layer separates them the other way. The rank-averaged AI risk score built from the three does not add to the traditional signals. These are the results to report; the exploratory section shows what a fitted score does, labelled as such.

A related-party amount is stated in about 28 % of fraud firm-years and 9–10 % of matched firm-years for lending and payables (Fisher p < 0.001). The H1 subset is therefore selected on the outcome: fraud firms disclose amounts more often, and H1 is tested only among firm-years that do. The same holds for the board share (25 % vs 13 %). Sales and purchases do not differ. This must be stated with every H1 result.

Fraud firm-years draw more often on the financial-statement note and matched firm-years more often on the Item 13 / proxy section; the share whose text states that there were no related-party transactions is similar (6 % vs 5 %).

Incompleteness when a firm discloses no RPT. The coder reads the related-party text of the firm-year. If the text is shorter than 20 words or absent, RP_INCOMP is missing (not scored). If the text exists but says there were no transactions, the four items are scored on that text: typically no counterparty, no amount, no terms, no purpose, so RP_INCOMP is high (mean 0.48 among such firm-years against 0.29 for others). That is a coding convention, not a disclosure failure, and it could produce the H2 reversal mechanically. The robustness run in §4.4 therefore drops those firm-years: the reversal shrinks and is no longer significant (OR 0.85, p = 0.240), so part of the H2 result is the convention and the rest is a null.

7. H2 and H5 — what the results mean and the options (for discussion; nothing changed)

H2. Fraud firms' related-party text is, if anything, more complete and plainer than their matched firms'. Three readings, none of which changes the hypothesis: (i) the efficient-transaction prediction set out in design §11.1 — disclosure characteristics do not mark harmful RPTs; (ii) part of the reversal is the coding convention for 'no transactions' texts (§4.1); dropping those firm-years leaves a null; (iii) fraud firms have related-party transactions to describe, matched firms often do not, so completeness partly measures the presence of RPTs. The thesis can report (i) as the finding and (ii)–(iii) as the limits of the measure. If the researcher wants a disclosure measure that is not confounded by presence, it would be a new exploratory measure, labelled as such.

H5. The rank-averaged score mixes a layer that works in the predicted direction (governance), one that is observed in a third of firm-years (financial) and one that works against the prediction (disclosure); averaged, it does not add to the four traditional signals. Options for discussion: (a) report as is — the honest outcome of the design; (b) a pre-specified variant in the thesis's robustness section, e.g. the governance layer alone against the traditional index, clearly labelled as post hoc; (c) the fitted score as exploratory evidence that a model with more inputs separates fraud firms, without claiming added value over the benchmark. The assistant recommends (a) with (c) as the exploratory section, and will not implement (b) without the researcher's decision.

8. Wording adopted throughout

  • Results are associations within matched sets; they identify fraud already under way, not fraud before it begins.
  • 'Lead time' is the earliest window year in which the measure separated the fraud firm from its matched firms, always reported beside the number of cases never flagged.
  • The 5 % matched-firm rate is a threshold fixed by construction, not an accuracy result; matched firms are not known to be clean.
  • 'Supported' means direction as predicted and Holm-adjusted p < 0.05 with the design's firm-clustered errors.
