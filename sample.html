Dataset and sample selection

From 58 candidate firms to 31 cases and 145 matched firms

Every candidate found in the enforcement record was screened by four rules; every case was matched by rule; every filing came from EDGAR. The screening report and the companies document hold the firm-by-firm detail.

Four rules turn 58 candidates into 31 cases

D1: the order describes a misstatement or misappropriation through an RPT, or a charge for not disclosing one. D2: a dated first public revelation. D3: at least four annual reports in the five years before it. D4: matched firms by rule. Counts are the screening file's own.

Four systematic sources

The client workbook was the starting list; full-text sweeps of every AAER and litigation release and EDGAR full-text search added the rest.

How the comparison firms were chosen

For each case: every EDGAR registrant with the same four-digit industry code (three- or two-digit when fewer than eight remain), a 10-K in every window year, no enforcement match, and XBRL total assets in the year before revelation; the five nearest in log assets, without replacement.

Five fiscal years before public disclosure

Event time counts fiscal years back from the revelation year. The misconduct period named in the enforcement document usually overlaps the window, so the study observes firms while the problem is under way, not before it begins.

Revelation years, 2010–2025

Each mark is one case at its revelation year; windows run back five fiscal years from here. Confidence in the date: high for a dated SEC filing or order, medium for a documented public event, low where the SEC action date stands in.
