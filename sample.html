Dataset and sample

From 61 candidate cases to 50 cases and 237 matched firms

Every candidate found in the enforcement record was screened by four rules; every case was matched by rule; every filing came from EDGAR.

A written entry rule turns 61 verified candidates into 50 cases

D1: the order describes a misstatement or misappropriation through a related-party transaction, or a charge for not disclosing one. D2: a dated first public revelation. D3: at least four annual reports in the five years before it. D4: matched firms by rule.

Entry tiers of the cases

A workbook of candidate firms was the starting list; full-text sweeps of every accounting enforcement release and litigation release, and EDGAR full-text search of issuers' own filings, added the rest.

How the comparison firms were chosen

For each case: every EDGAR registrant with the same four-digit industry code (three- or two-digit when fewer than eight remain), an annual report in every window year, no enforcement match, and XBRL total assets in the year before revelation; the five nearest in log assets, without replacement.

Five fiscal years before public disclosure

Event time counts fiscal years back from the revelation year. The misconduct period named in the enforcement document usually overlaps the window, so the study observes firms while the problem is under way, not before it begins.

Revelation years, 2005–2025

Each mark is one case at its revelation year; windows run back five fiscal years from here. Confidence in the date: high for a dated SEC filing or order, medium for a documented public event, low where the SEC action date stands in.

Scope of the sample

Size. 50 case firms is every verified case that passes the written entry rule (at least three annual reports in a window ending FY2004 or later); the within-set tests use informative matched sets, so non-significant results are weak evidence of no difference.

Two forms of case. 36 cases are misstatements or misappropriations carried out through a related-party transaction; 14 are charges for failing to disclose one. Both are observed case firms in every test.

Matched firms. Comparison firms are firms with no SEC accounting enforcement, not firms known to be clean. Sets in thin industries have fewer than five matched firms or a wider size gap.

Observation window. The five years before public disclosure usually include the years of the misconduct, so the study identifies firms while the problem is under way and before the public knew.
